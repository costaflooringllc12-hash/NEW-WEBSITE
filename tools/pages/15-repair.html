How a repair is done

Cut out, lace in, sand and finish as one floor.

  1. {{ICON:cut}}

    Cut out the damage

    Damaged boards are cut out cleanly along the grain.

  2. {{ICON:layers}}

    Lace in new boards

    Same species and width, staggered into the surrounding floor so there's no straight seam.

  3. {{ICON:sander}}

    Sand flat

    The repaired area, or usually the whole room, is sanded level.

  4. {{ICON:drop}}

    Match the color

    Stain matched to the existing floor, or the whole room refinished in a new one.

  5. {{ICON:brush}}

    Finish coats

    Finish goes on top and the repair reads as part of the floor.

For a repair to truly disappear, the whole room usually needs to be refinished. Spot-finishing a patch in the middle of a floor almost always shows. We'll be upfront about which option makes sense for your floor and budget.

Good to know

Old wood, matching stock, and the right time to repair.

{{ICON:search}}

Old wood, matching species

Older homes in Union and Essex counties often have narrow 2¼" red oak strip or old-growth pine. We source matching stock for these so the repair isn't a different width or grain.

{{ICON:clock}}

Repair after a water event, not before

Wood needs to dry out fully, sometimes several weeks, before it's repaired and refinished, or the new boards will cup. We can inspect early, tell you what to expect, and schedule the work once moisture readings come down.
